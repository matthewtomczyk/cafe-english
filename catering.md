# Services in a Cafe

#### A manager's morning: opening the cafe

I arrive at the cafe at 6:00 every morning, before the first customers. The first thing I do is turn on the ovens and check the temperature. Then I switch on the espresso machine so that it has time to heat up. Next, I check the fridges. The milk, cream and butter should be at the right temperature, and I write the readings in the log book.

The bakery delivers the croissants, muffins and pain au chocolat at 6:30, but the trays need to be arranged in the display case before 7:00. I check each item carefully. Anything that is burnt, cracked or past its best goes in the bin. Freshly baked pastries go on the top shelf, and the older ones go at the back, so that we sell them first.

At 6:45 the morning team arrives. I brief them for five minutes: who is on the coffee machine, who is on the till, and who looks after the display case. I also remind them of today's special, the cinnamon roll, and of the gluten-free options, because two regular customers always ask for them. Then I restock the cups, lids and napkins.

By 7:30 the first customers are waiting at the door. Most of them order a flat white and a pastry to take away. During the rush I help behind the counter, because the baristas should not feel alone when the queue is long. Yesterday a customer complained that his cappuccino was cold and his croissant was stale. I apologised, made him a new drink free of charge, and checked the display case again.

At 9:00 the rush is over. I count the cash in the till, sign the cash-up sheet, and write a list of what we need for the afternoon: milk, sugar, paper cups and two more trays of pain au chocolat.

**shift** – a period of time during which a person works

**log book** – a book in which records are written down

**display case** – a glass cabinet in which food is shown to customers

**to brief** – to give somebody short instructions or information before a task

**to restock** – to fill up again with items that have been used or sold

**rush** – a busy period when many customers arrive at the same time

**queue** – a line of people waiting for something

**stale** – not fresh

**to apologise** – to say sorry

**cash-up** – the process of counting the money in the till at the end of a shift

#### Coffee

Coffee is a popular drink made from roasted coffee beans. It is grown in more than 70 countries, mainly in South America, Southeast Asia, India and Africa. When the beans are ripe, they are picked, dried and sold.

Green (unroasted) beans are traded all over the world. After that, they are roasted to get the right flavour, then they are ground and brewed. There are two main types of coffee beans: Arabica, which is mild and popular, and Robusta, which is stronger and cheaper.

The drink. Coffee is one of the most popular drinks in the world. It contains caffeine, a chemical that gives you energy and keeps you awake. Many people drink it every day. Studies show that drinking coffee in moderation is not harmful for healthy adults.

History. Coffee was first grown in Yemen, in the Middle East. People drank it in religious ceremonies in the 15th century. The Christian church did not like this, so coffee was banned in Ethiopia for many years. It was also banned in Turkey in the 17th century for political reasons, and in Europe it was sometimes seen as a rebellious drink.

Economy. Coffee is an important export product for many countries. Green (unroasted) coffee is one of the most traded agricultural products in the world. Coffee growing can harm the environment, so more and more farmers now grow organic coffee.

**roast** – to cook food without water, usually in a hot oven, until it turns brown

**to grow** – to plant crops and look after them until they are ready

**ripe** – ready to be picked and used

**trade** – to buy and sell goods, especially between countries

**mild** – not strong in taste or smell

**cheap** – not expensive

**caffeine** – the chemical in coffee that gives you energy

**to ban** – to say that something is not allowed

**export** – to send goods to another country to sell them

**environment** – the air, water, land and animals around us

**organic** – grown without chemicals and artificial fertilisers

# Services in a Restaurant

#### Breakfast

One Sunday morning, my family and I went to a popular restaurant for breakfast. As soon as we walked into Restaurant Happy Meal, a young waitress showed us to a table. We ordered our food and soon after, our food came. Just as we were about to begin our meal, we heard someone shouting for the proprietor.

"What is this? A dead fly in my food!" a man with a deep scar across his face roared angrily. He banged his fist on the table and swept the plates and cups off it. These came crashing to the floor, breaking into pieces. His companion, a huge man with a tattoo on his arm, stared angrily at the proprietor who stood nervously in front of the two gangsters.

The proprietor apologized repeatedly to the angry customers and tried to pacify them by offering to replace their food. He even told them that they could have their meal for free.

The commotion affected the business of the restaurant. Fearing that a brawl would ensue, many customers quickly paid for their meals and left the restaurant hurriedly. Some of them had not even finished their breakfast.

My father told us to eat our food quickly and not to look at the two angry men. We obeyed him and finished our food within minutes. That was probably the quietest and fastest breakfast my family ever had.

Although my father warned us not to look at the two unruly customers, I could not help stealing a glance at them.

I saw that the proprietor had managed to pacify the two angry customers. Fresh food and drinks were brought to their table. They sat down again and continued their breakfast. Meanwhile, the waitress who had shown us to our table earlier swept up the broken china.

**proprietor** – the owner of a business establishment, a hotel, etc.

**scar** – a mark left by a healed wound, sore, or burn

**to sweep** – to move or remove (dust, dirt, etc.) with or as if with a broom or brush

**to pacify** – to bring or restore to a state of peace or calm

**commotion** – violent or tumultuous motion; agitation; noisy disturbance

**brawl** – an angry, rough, noisy fight, especially one under the influence of alcohol

**to ensue** – to follow in order; come afterward, especially in immediate succession

**hurriedly** – in a way that is done very quickly or too quickly

**unruly** – difficult to control; not obeying rules

**to glance** – to give a quick, short look

**china** – high-quality clay shaped and heated to make it hard, or objects made from it, such as cups and plates

#### What a British Person Should Know When Eating Out in America

Eating out may be a simple thing, but for British people who have just come to America, it could be an experience full of surprises. Here are five main differences a Brit should pay attention to when eating out in a restaurant in America.

**Ordering drinks.** The first thing a waiter would ask when you sit down in an American restaurant is whether you would like bottled or tap water. If you choose bottled, you will then be asked whether you would prefer still or sparkling. Soon, the water you ordered will be brought to you. Coffee and soft drinks will also be gladly refilled. The waiter will proactively ask if you want another coffee or Coke. In Britain, water and soft drinks must be requested and they are not free.

**The service.** Waiters in American restaurants are more pleasant and attentive. They are always chatty and often come to your table during the meal to refill drinks and check everything is okay. In England, waiters are less likely to frequently check on customers. If you need something, you can raise your hand and wave at the waiter or else you will be left alone for hours.

**The menu.** In an American restaurant, picky eaters can customize the menu to suit their exact preferences by reeling off special requirements and substitutions. You can order salad but without tomato or with a special dressing to accommodate your diet. There is a friendly atmosphere that encourages you to have exactly what you want and satisfy your appetite. On the other hand, people in England tend to order a meal with all the options that come with it. It is less common for them to complain about the food or have the waiter send dishes back to the kitchen.

**Picking up the bill.** In America, a waiter will bring you the bill even though you have not asked for it. This would be seen as very impolite by British customers, as it implies that they are being hurried out of the restaurant.

**Tipping.** Tipping in England is optional. You will tip if you are satisfied with the meal and service, which is generally about 10%. If you are dissatisfied, you do not need to tip and this is quite common. In America, a tip of 15–20% is generally mandatory. For American waiters, tipping is the main source of their income. Even if the service is bad you should still tip, but it makes sense to give a smaller tip.

**tap** – a device that controls the flow of liquid, especially water, from a pipe

**sparkling** – a sparkling drink contains many small bubbles of gas

**proactively** – in a way that causes change by taking action, rather than reacting to changes

**chatty** – liking to talk a lot in a friendly, informal way

**to waive** – to dismiss with or as if with a wave of the hand

**to reel off** – to recite or write fluently and without apparent effort

**to imply** – to indicate or suggest without being explicitly stated

# Services in a Bar

#### Alcohol and Young People

You can be stopped, fined or arrested by police if you're under 18 and drinking alcohol in public.

If you're under 18, it's against the law:
- for someone to sell you alcohol
- to buy or try to buy alcohol
- for an adult to buy or try to buy alcohol for you
- to drink alcohol in licensed premises (e.g. a pub or restaurant)

However, if you're 16 or 17 and accompanied by an adult, you can drink (but not buy) beer, wine or cider with a meal. If you're 16 or under, you may be able to go to a pub (or premises primarily used to sell alcohol) if you're accompanied by an adult. However, this isn't always the case. It can also depend on the specific conditions for that premises.

It's illegal to give alcohol to children under 5.

Working in a restaurant or bar under 18: You can serve alcohol in a restaurant if you're 16 or 17 as long as the alcohol is drunk with a meal. You can also serve alcohol in a bar, but the licence holder or bar manager needs to approve the sale. You may be restricted to selling alcohol in sealed containers (e.g. cans or bottles) in some areas.

**fine** – a sum of money imposed as punishment for an offence

**premises** – the land and buildings owned by someone, especially by a company or organisation

**primarily** – essentially; mostly; chiefly; at first; originally

**licence** – an official document that gives you permission to own, do, or use something

**to approve** – to have a positive opinion of someone or something

**to restrict** – to limit the movements or actions of someone, or to limit or prevent something

**can** – a closed metal container, especially cylinder-shaped, in which some types of drink and food are sold

#### Unit 1 – Services in a Cafe

**American roast** – a medium-brown coffee roast, also known as "City Roast"; traditionally preferred in the U.S.

**black coffee** – coffee served without cream or milk

**cafe** – a coffee shop or a small restaurant where coffee is served

**cafeteria** – a restaurant where you serve yourself, then pay a cashier

**caffeine** – a chemical, found in coffee, that stimulates the central nervous system and body metabolism

**chaff** – flakes of the innermost skin (silverskin) of the coffee fruit that clings to green coffee and loosens during roasting

**cherry** – the fruit of the coffee tree; each cherry contains two coffee beans or one "peaberry"

**clean** – a term used to describe coffee with no discernible defects in its flavour

**coffee beans** – the seed of the coffee plant, picked, processed, and dried once ripe

**dark roast** – coffee beans roasted to a medium-dark colour and beyond; coffee oils appear on the surface

**decaffeinated coffee** – coffee that has undergone a process to remove caffeine from the beans

**drip coffee** – also called filtered coffee; made by pouring water over roasted, ground coffee beans in a filter

**earthiness** – a taste found mainly in coffees from Sumatra and Sulawesi, where wet coffee has come into contact with earth during drying

**espresso** – strong, dark coffee made by forcing a small amount of nearly boiling water under pressure through finely ground coffee

**flat white** – a coffee beverage made by pouring microfoam over a single or double shot of espresso

**full-city roast** – a medium-dark roast which produces some oil on the bean surface; sweetness and body increase, acidity diminishes

**green coffee** – unroasted coffee beans

**hard bean** – coffee grown at altitudes of 4,000–4,500 feet; a harder, less porous bean

**instant coffee** – coffee dried into soluble powder or freeze-dried into granules that dissolve in hot water

**Italian roast** – a very dark roast, rich and bittersweet in flavour

**latte** – a coffee drink made with espresso and steamed milk

**long black** – a double shot of espresso poured over hot water

**micro lot** – coffee from a single farm or a specific part of a coffee farm

**mocha** – either a rich, pungent Arabian coffee, or a coffee drink flavoured with milk, sugar, and cocoa

**monsooned coffee** – dry-processed, single-origin coffee from India, exposed to monsoon winds to increase body and reduce acidity

**quakers** – defective coffee beans that fail to roast properly and remain pale-coloured

**robusta** – a low-growing, high-yield coffee tree; full-bodied but bland coffee of inferior quality, used in cheap blends and instant coffee

**silverskin** – the thin, innermost skin of the coffee fruit which clings to dried beans until it is polished off or floats free during roasting

**single-origin coffee** – unblended coffee from a single country, region, and crop

**skinny coffee** – coffee with skimmed milk

**white coffee** – regular black coffee with milk, cream or another "whitener" added

**acidity** – the sharp, lively quality of high-grown coffee, tasted at the tip of the tongue; not the same as bitter or sour

**aeropress** – a device for brewing coffee; coffee is steeped and then forced through a filter by pressing the plunger

**Americano** – a shot or two of espresso poured into a cup, which is then filled with hot water

**aroma** – the fragrance of brewed coffee

**Arabica** – the earliest cultivated species of coffee tree; about 70% of the world's coffee; superior in cup quality

**barista** – a person who makes coffee drinks as a profession

**batch roaster** – a machine that roasts a given quantity of coffee at a time

**blade grinder** – a device that uses a propeller-like blade to grind coffee

**bitter** – the taste perceived at the back of the tongue; dark roasts are intentionally bitter

**blend** – a mixture of two or more varietals of coffee

**briny** – a salty sensation caused by coffee being left on a warming element too long after brewing

**burr grinder** – a coffee grinder with two adjustable shredding discs or burrs

**caffè Americano** – a style of coffee prepared by adding hot water to espresso

**café au lait** – coffee combining one-third drip-brewed coffee with two-thirds hot, frothed milk

**caffè macchiato** – an espresso with a small amount of milk added; literally "stained coffee"

**cappuccino** – an Italian coffee drink traditionally prepared with espresso, hot milk and steamed milk foam

**clever coffee dripper** – a filter cone with a stopper, which allows coffee to steep before dripping

**clover brewer** – a high-tech, high-end, single-cup brewing machine

**cortado** – a Spanish term for an espresso topped with a small amount of flat steamed milk

**cupping** – tasting coffee (as "tasting" is used for wine)

**crema** – the caramel-coloured foam that appears on top of a shot of espresso during brewing

**drip method** – a brewing method in which hot water settles through a bed of ground coffee

**exotic** – unusual aromatic and flavour notes, such as berry or floral

**extraction** – using water just off the boil (195–205 °F) to draw flavour from coffee grounds

**French press** – a device for making coffee in which ground coffee is steeped in water and pressed to the bottom with a filter plunger; also called plunger pot or cafetière

**froth or foam** – milk made thick and foamy by aerating it with hot steam

**redeye** – a cup of brewed coffee with a shot of espresso

**spicy** – having the flavour of particular spices

#### Unit 2 – Services in a Restaurant

**à la carte** – (of a menu) having dishes listed separately and individually priced

**appetizer** – a small snack before a meal

**awful (taste)** – a very bad taste

**beverage** – any drink, especially one other than water, such as tea, coffee, beer, or milk

**bill, cheque, check** – the slip of paper that tells the customer how much to pay

**bread basket** – slices of bread and butter served before and with a meal

**buffet** – self-serve tables of food set out for customers to pick from

**cheese platter** – a selection of cheeses and crackers served on a large plate

**chef** – a staff member professionally trained to prepare food

**complaint** – an expression of discontent, regret, or fault-finding

**complimentary** – given or supplied without charge

**counter** – a flat area for placing dishes on or preparing food

**course** – one part of a meal

**cuisine** – a country or region's style of cooking

**deep fried** – cooked in a large pot of oil

**dessert** – cake, pie, fruit, pudding, ice cream, etc., served as the final course of a meal

**eat out** (phrasal verb) – to go out to a restaurant, especially at night

**entrée, main course** – the largest part of a meal (after the appetizer, before dessert)

**fast food** – quickly prepared food such as hamburgers and French fries

**high-class** – of high quality or high social status

**hors d'oeuvre** – a savoury food served as an appetizer before the main course

**garnish** – food added to a plate for appearance or colour (e.g. parsley or fruit)

**happy hour** – a short time when alcoholic drinks are on sale

**ingredients** – all the different foods that are combined in a recipe

**make a booking / book a table** – to contact a restaurant and reserve a table for a meal

**menu** – the list of foods and drinks served in a restaurant, café, hotel, etc.

**non-alcoholic beverage** – a drink that resembles an alcoholic drink but has no alcohol

**pack up** (verb) – to put food in a take-out container for the customer to take home

**pasta** – Italian food made from flour and water, such as spaghetti, ravioli, lasagne

**portion** – a separate amount of food

**pudding** – a soft, sweet food eaten as a dessert

**punch in / punch out** – to put a card in a punch clock to mark the start and end of a shift

**recipe** – a list of ingredients and instructions for preparing a certain type of food

**regulars** – people who come into an establishment often

**reservation** – a request to have a table saved for a certain number of people

**restaurant** – a place with a kitchen and dining area where people pay to eat meals

**sauce** – a liquid flavouring added to food

**savoury** – (of food) salty or spicy rather than sweet

**service charge** – an extra amount added to a restaurant bill, meant to be given to staff for their service

**set menu** – a multi-course meal sold for a fixed price

**side dish** – an extra dish or serving ordered together with a main dish

**specialty** – a food item that a restaurant is popular for

**tip** (noun) – extra money given by a guest to a waiter or other staff member

**tip** (verb) – to give extra money to a waiter or other staff member for their service

**vegetarian** – (of dishes or diets) not including animal products, especially meat

**waiter (male) / waitress (female)** – a person who takes orders from guests and brings their food

**take-out** – food that is packed up to be eaten at home

**well-done** – cooked for longer than average

**wine list** – a printed list of all the wines available in a restaurant

**cobbler shaker** – an all-in-one cocktail shaker of three parts: a metal tin, a cap, and a built-in strainer

**cocktail straw** – a long, slim straw used for sipping and stirring cocktails and other drinks

**coffee machine** – a small electric machine that makes coffee; a coffeemaker

**condiment** – a substance, such as salt, that you add to food to improve its taste

**copper mug** – a copper cup used to serve drinks such as ginger ale, iced tea, and Moscow Mules

**corkcrew** – a tool with a pointed metal helix that is screwed into a cork and pulled to extract it from a bottle

**coupette** – a small coupe glass used for serving cold seafood, fruit, or ices

**domestic** – belonging or relating to the home, house, or family; (of beer) made in the country where the bar is located

**draught beer (BrE) / draft beer (AmE)** – beer drawn from a keg

**flair bottle** – a bottle designed to imitate a liquor bottle, so that bartenders can practise tricks without breaking glass

**highball / Collins** – a tall glass (about 8–12 oz) used to serve mixed drinks such as a tequila sunrise or long island iced tea

**hurricane glass** – a tall drinking glass, usually holding about 20 US fluid ounces, used for mixed drinks such as the Hurricane

**ice bucket** – a container for ice, often with tongs and a strainer, used when serving at the table

**ice cube machine** – a small machine that makes and stores ice cubes continuously

**ingredients** – foods used with other foods in the preparation of a particular dish or drink

**jigger** – a measure for liquor, usually 1–2 oz (30–60 ml), with a large side and a small side

**lager** – a beer brewed by slow fermentation and matured under refrigeration

**last orders (BrE) / last call (AmE)** – the last drinks customers may buy before the bar closes

**Martini glass** – a stemmed glass with an inverted cone bowl, used for martinis and many other cocktails

**mason jar** – a glass jar used in home canning, named after John Landis Mason; often used as a drinking glass

**muddler** – a tool used to mash fruit, herbs, and spices in the bottom of a glass to release their flavour

**mug** – a heavy glass with a handle, out of which you drink beer

**neat** – not diluted or mixed with other substances

**offer** – to put forward or suggest for acceptance or rejection

**on the rocks** – served with ice cubes

**order** – to request that something be supplied

**pitcher** – a container with a handle and a lip for pouring liquids (BrE: jug)

**potent** – very powerful, forceful, or effective

**pour** – to make a liquid flow out of a container, especially into another

**recipe** – a set of instructions for preparing and cooking food, including a list of ingredients

**recommend** – to advise, counsel, or suggest

**refreshing** – making you feel less hot or tired

**run a tab** – to accumulate charges on a bill at a bar or tavern

**serve** – to offer or set food or drink before a person

**shot** – a small amount of an alcoholic drink

**stirrer** – an implement for stirring a drink

**strainer** – a kitchen utensil with holes for separating liquid from solids
